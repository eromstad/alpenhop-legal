Terms of Use

The game

Alpenhop is an arcade game: hop a mountain goat up an alpine mountain, lane by lane, past rolling boulders, skiers, lifts, ice bridges and avalanches, to the summit. It has a Daily Summit that is the same mountain for every player each day, and Expeditions that climb peak after peak ahead of a rising cloud. Edelweiss picked on the way can be spent on new climbers and collar bells in the Locker. By using Alpenhop, you agree to these terms.

The free game and Premium

Alpenhop is free to download. The Daily Summit is free for everyone, every day, and so are the last seven days of past mountains. Expeditions can be played free for their first two peaks. The free game shows ads (see "Advertising" in the Privacy Policy).

Alpenhop Premium is a single one-time purchase, never a subscription. It unlocks full Expeditions and every past Daily Summit, and removes all ads. Nothing sold in Alpenhop makes the Daily Summit easier: it is the same climb for everyone. Climbers and bells in the Locker are earned with edelweiss picked in the game and change only how your goat looks and sounds.

  • Payment is charged to your Apple ID at confirmation of purchase. The price is shown in the App Store before you buy.
  • Premium is yours for good on the account you bought it with. If you reinstall the app or move to a new device, use Restore purchases (in Settings and on the Premium card) to get it back.
  • Payments are processed entirely by Apple; refunds are handled under Apple's standard policies, not by Alpenhop's developer directly.

Leaderboards and fair play

Daily Summit times and Expedition scores can appear on Game Center leaderboards. Don't use cheats, modified copies of the app or other tricks to post times or scores you didn't climb for; results that look impossible may be removed.

Privacy Policy

The short version: Alpenhop has no user accounts and no server of its own. Your progress, purchase, streaks, edelweiss and settings stay on your device. Two things aren't purely local: the free game shows ads through Google's Mobile Ads SDK, which can use an advertising identifier only if you agree to it; and if you're signed in to Game Center, your Daily Summit times and Expedition scores go to it. Both are explained below, with how to decline.

Information we collect

None, on our own servers. Alpenhop has no server of its own and uses no analytics or crash-reporting SDKs. The third parties it works with are the advertising SDK, Game Center and the App Store, each described below.

Advertising

Alpenhop's free version shows ads through Google's Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob): an occasional full-screen ad between climbs (never during your first climbs), and a short ad you can choose to watch for one more heart when you run out on a Daily Summit. There are no banner ads, and the rewarded ad is always optional.

  • Your consent comes first. Where the law requires it (such as in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), Alpenhop shows Google's consent form before any ads, and you can change your answer anytime in Settings → Privacy choices.
  • On iPhone, the app asks for App Tracking Transparency permission before any tracking-based ad targeting, as Apple requires. You can allow or decline in the system prompt, and Alpenhop plays exactly the same either way.
  • Personalised only with your yes. Ads are personalised only if you agree in both places that apply to you. If you decline, ads are requested as non-personalised: they aren't targeted by your interests. Even then, the ad SDK may use your device's advertising identifier to limit how often an ad repeats, prevent fraud and measure ads. You can reset your advertising identifier or turn off tracking in your device's settings.

Google's use of any data collected through the SDK is governed by Google's own privacy policy and its disclosures for how it uses information from apps that use its services, not by Alpenhop's developer. Alpenhop Premium removes ads (and this SDK's ad activity) entirely; Premium players are never asked about ad consent or tracking.

Game Center

Alpenhop signs in to Apple's Game Center if you use it. When you're signed in, your Daily Summit times and Expedition scores are sent to Game Center, and your results and player name can appear on its leaderboards for other players to see. Game Center is run by Apple under its own privacy policy. If you're not signed in, Alpenhop keeps all of this on your device only. You can sign out in your device's Game Center settings.

Your data

  • Local storage only. Your best times, streaks, logbook, edelweiss, climbers and bells, Premium, and your settings (sound, graphics, accessibility, Relaxed mode and privacy choices) live in your device's local storage.
  • Deleting your data. Uninstalling the app permanently deletes everything on your device. There is no account to delete and no server-side copy, because none exists. Premium can be restored on a reinstall from your Apple account; anything sent to Game Center is managed there.

Sharing a result

When you share a climb, Alpenhop makes the result card (an image and a short text summary) entirely on your device and hands it to whatever app you pick in your device's own share sheet (Messages, email, and so on). Alpenhop never uploads it anywhere.

In-app purchase

Premium is bought through Apple's standard in-app purchase system. Alpenhop's developer never receives your payment details (card numbers, billing address, and so on) — that's handled directly between you and Apple under its own privacy policy. Alpenhop only learns whether you own Premium, so it can unlock it on your device.

Ratings

After a summit, Alpenhop may ask whether you'd like to rate it, using Apple's built-in rating prompt. Apple handles the prompt and any rating you leave; Alpenhop doesn't see your answer.

Children's privacy

Alpenhop does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children. The app has no accounts, no chat, and no messaging between players; the only third-party data collection is the advertising SDK and Game Center described above, which are limited by Apple's own platform rules for apps used by a general audience.
